Transitioning from traditional paper drawing to a digital tablet can feel overwhelming. Infinite brush choices, complex menus, and layer panels can easily crowd your creative thoughts.

However, once you strip away the secondary features and focus on core concepts, digital illustration becomes a fast and incredibly flexible tool.

## 1. Canvas Setup and Resolution Rules

Before starting, you must configure your canvas correctly. The two values you need to know are:

*   **Pixel Dimensions:** For standard web and social media sharing, a canvas of 2000px to 3000px on the longest side is perfect.
*   **DPI (Dots Per Inch):** If you ever plan to print your artwork, your DPI must be set to 300. For web-only display, 72 DPI is technically sufficient, but keeping it at 300 DPI gives you future flexibility.

Always work in RGB color mode for digital displays, and convert to CMYK only when sending a design to a physical print shop.

## 2. The Layer System and Non-Destructive Editing

Layers are the primary advantage of digital art. They allow you to modify parts of your drawing without affecting the rest of the canvas. Build a standard layer stack:

1.  **Background Color Layer:** Usually set to a warm cream or off-white. Avoid stark pure white as it causes eye strain.
2.  **Sketch Layer:** Set to 30% opacity at the bottom of your stack.
3.  **Color Base Layer:** Block in your solid shapes (flat colors). Keep separate elements (like hair, skin, clothes) on separate layers.
4.  **Shadows and Highlights Layer:** Clip these to your color base layers so you never paint outside the boundaries.

## 3. Mastering Blending Modes

Blending modes determine how a layer interacts with the layers beneath it. While there are dozens of options, you only need to master three:

*   **Multiply:** Darkens the colors below. Perfect for painting shadows.
*   **Screen:** Lightens the colors below. Great for adding glows and light beams.
*   **Overlay:** Enhances contrast and saturation. Ideal for adding ambient light changes.

  "Digital tools give you infinite retries. Use them to experiment courageously with values and color harmony."

## 4. Mimicking Traditional Textures Digitally

A common criticism of digital art is that it can look too clinical or flat. To add warmth and an organic feel, introduce paper textures:

Find a high-resolution photograph of textured watercolor paper or canvas. Place this image at the very top of your layer stack. Set the blending mode to **Multiply** or **Overlay**, and lower the opacity to around 10% to 15%. 

This overlay introduces subtle paper grain across all colors, instantly making your digital illustration feel tactile, premium, and traditional.
