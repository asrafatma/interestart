# Easy Air Dry Clay Crafts: 17 Cute Clay Bag Charms to Make

Bag charms are having a real moment. Scroll through any tote bag, backpack, or crossbody purse right now and there's a decent chance something small and handmade is dangling off the zipper. Some people are paying a lot of money for that little detail. You don't have to. A block of air dry clay, a few basic tools, and an afternoon on your kitchen table will get you further than most of what's sold online, and the result actually looks like *you* made it, which is kind of the point.

I've been making these on and off for the past few months, mostly as a way to use up scrap clay from bigger projects, and they've become the thing I reach for whenever I want a low-pressure creative win. No kiln, no polymer clay oven curing, no wheel. Just clay, your hands, and paint. If you've never sculpted anything before, this is genuinely one of the friendliest crafts to start with.

Below you'll find everything you need to get going, a quick rundown of the actual technique, and then 17 charm ideas ranging from five-minute shapes to slightly more involved ones you'll want to save for a rainy afternoon.

## What You'll Need

You don't need a full pottery studio for this. Here's the realistic list:

- **White air dry clay** – smooth, fine-grained brands work best for small detail work. Avoid anything with visible fiber texture; it tends to crack on thin edges.
- **Clay sculpting tools** – a basic five-piece wooden or silicone set covers you for shaping, smoothing, and texturing.
- **A small rolling pin or glass jar** – for flattening clay evenly.
- **Cookie cutters or a craft knife** – mini cutters make quick work of consistent shapes like circles, hearts, and stars.
- **Eye pins or jump rings** – inserted before the clay dries, these give you something to actually attach the charm to a bag.
- **Acrylic or gouache paint, plus fine detail brushes** – gouache in particular dries to a soft, almost velvety matte finish that photographs beautifully.
- **A glossy sealant** – a resin coat, UV resin, or even a few thin layers of Mod Podge will protect your paint job and give the charm a slight shine.
- **Sandpaper (fine grit)** – for smoothing rough edges once the clay is bone dry.

Everything on that list is inexpensive and widely available at craft stores, so this isn't a project that requires a big upfront investment before you know if you even enjoy it.

## The Basic Technique, Start to Finish

Every charm on this list follows roughly the same process, just with different shapes. Once you've made one, the rest go faster.

**1. Condition the clay.** Knead it in your hands for a minute or two until it's soft and pliable, with no cracks or dry patches.

**2. Roll and shape.** Flatten with a rolling pin for cutter-based shapes, or hand-sculpt for more organic forms like fruit or flowers. Aim for a thickness of around 4-6mm; thinner pieces dry faster but are more fragile.

**3. Insert your hardware.** Before the clay sets, push an eye pin or a bent piece of wire into the top edge. This is the step people skip and regret. Do it now, not after drying.

**4. Let it dry completely.** Most thin charms are dry within 24 hours, but give thicker or layered pieces up to 48. Don't rush this part with a hairdryer or oven; the clay can warp or crack.

**5. Sand lightly.** Once fully dry, a quick pass with fine sandpaper smooths any rough edges or fingerprints.

**6. Prime, then paint.** A thin coat of white gesso or acrylic gives you a clean base and helps colors stay true. Let it dry before adding your design.

**7. Seal it.** This step matters more than people think. Air dry clay is porous and will absorb dirt and moisture over time if left unsealed, especially on something that's going to bump around on the outside of a bag all day. A glossy resin dip or a few coats of a durable sealant will make the difference between a charm that lasts a season and one that lasts for years.

With the basics down, here are 17 designs worth trying, roughly ordered from simplest to slightly more advanced.

## 1. Smiley Sun Charm

A rounded circle with short, slightly curved triangle rays pressed around the edge and a simple smiling face painted on in black. This one is deceptively forgiving; even a slightly wobbly sun still reads as charming rather than messy. Keep the rays short and blunt rather than sharp and pointy so they don't snap off once dry.

## 2. Daisy Flower Cluster

Roll five or six small teardrop shapes, flatten them, and arrange them petal-side-out around a tiny ball of clay for the center. Making a cluster of two or three daisies at slightly different sizes, joined at the base, reads far more interesting than one flower alone. Paint the centers a warm mustard yellow against soft white petals.

![Handmade air dry clay smiley sun charm and daisy flower cluster charm side by side](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/sun-daisy-charms.webp)

## 3. Mini Mushroom

Two simple pieces: a rounded dome for the cap and a short cylinder for the stem, pressed together while still soft. Add tiny painted dots on the cap once dry for that classic toadstool look, or keep it plain in a warm terracotta for something more subdued. Mushrooms are one of the fastest shapes on this list to sculpt.

## 4. Strawberry Charm

Shape a small cone with slightly rounded edges, texture the surface with the tip of a needle tool for the seed indentations, and top it with a tiny green calyx sculpted separately and pressed on before drying. Paint the body in a warm coral-red and add small yellow seed dots once the base coat is dry.

![Handmade air dry clay mini mushroom charm and strawberry charm on a cream surface](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/mushroom-strawberry-charms.webp)

## 5. Citrus Slice

Roll a flattened circle, then use a fine tool to score wedge lines radiating from the center like a pie, mimicking the segments of an orange or lemon slice. A thin rolled coil pressed around the outer edge gives you the peel. This one looks intricate but is really just careful line work with a tool tip.

## 6. Wavy-Edge Heart

Instead of a smooth heart cutter, hand-shape the outline with a slightly uneven, wavy edge. It sounds like a small detail, but it's what separates a handmade-looking charm from something that reads as stamped out of a mold. Keep the color palette soft, dusty rose rather than bright red, to match a more editorial aesthetic.

![Handmade air dry clay citrus slice charm and wavy-edge heart charm styled together](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/citrus-heart-charms.webp)

## 7. Cloud with a Rainbow Arc

Sculpt a puffy, rounded cloud shape, then press a thin arched strip of clay along the bottom edge before it dries, painting it in soft pastel bands once everything has cured. This is one of the more time-consuming shapes on the list simply because of the arc placement, but it's also one of the most eye-catching finished pieces.

## 8. Butterfly Charm

Cut two matching wing shapes, join them at a narrow central body, and use a needle tool to score simple vein lines into each wing before the clay sets. Painting the wings in a gradient, dusty lavender fading to cream, gives this one a lot more depth than a flat single color would.

![Handmade air dry clay cloud with rainbow arc charm and butterfly charm](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/cloud-rainbow-butterfly-charms.webp)

## 9. Coastal Shell or Starfish

Press a small clay ball into a real scallop shell (lightly oiled first so it releases cleanly) to pick up natural ridges and texture, or hand-shape a five-point starfish with a rounded, slightly uneven silhouette. This is the design that consistently shows up in coastal-themed craft content right now, and it's easy to see why. It looks far more detailed than the actual sculpting time it takes.

## 10. Ladybug Charm

A rounded dome base painted red, with a black painted head and a fine painted line down the center back to suggest folded wings, finished with small black dots. Because most of the character here comes from paint rather than sculpting, this is a great option if your hand-shaping skills are still developing but your painting is solid.

![Handmade air dry clay coastal shell charm and ladybug charm on linen](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/coastal-shell-ladybug-charms.webp)

## 11. Tiny Teapot

Shape a small rounded body, add a thin coil handle and a short spout, and don't skip the tiny lid knob on top; it's what sells the silhouette. This one takes more patience than most on this list because of the number of small attached pieces, but it's also one of the most satisfying to finish.

## 12. Avocado Charm

Sculpt a classic avocado-half silhouette, hollow the center slightly with a rounded tool, and press in a smaller ball for the pit once the surrounding clay has firmed up a little. Two-tone painting, cream flesh fading into a deep sage skin, is what makes this one look intentional rather than like an unidentifiable green blob.

![Handmade air dry clay mini teapot charm and avocado charm styled together](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/teapot-avocado-charms.webp)

## 13. Cherry Duo

Two small rounded balls joined by a thin, slightly curved stem, painted in a deep warm red with a single small highlight dot on each cherry to suggest shine. Keep the stem thin but not fragile; a slight curve rather than a straight line makes it look more natural.

## 14. Bow Charm

Two folded triangle-ish loops pinched at the center with a small wrapped band of clay, similar to a real ribbon bow. This shape is more about patience than skill; getting both loops roughly symmetrical takes a couple of tries, but even a slightly imperfect bow still reads clearly.

![Handmade air dry clay cherry duo charm and bow charm on a cream surface](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/cherry-bow-charms.webp)

## 15. Star Cluster

Three or four small stars at varying sizes, sculpted with soft rounded points rather than sharp ones so they hold up better over time, grouped and joined at their bases into a single charm. This one works especially well as a companion piece alongside a moon or cloud charm if you're building out a themed set.

## 16. Mini Coffee Cup

A short cylinder body with a thin coil handle, topped with a slightly domed "foam" layer in a contrasting cream tone. Small painted details, a thin heart or leaf shape on top, give it a latte-art feel without requiring any extra sculpting.

## 17. Organic Wavy Blob

Not everything needs to be a recognizable object. A simple, smooth, asymmetrical blob shape, sanded until it's satin-smooth, then painted in a single warm solid color, taps into the abstract, sculptural charm trend that's been showing up alongside the cuter kawaii styles. It's also the fastest shape on this entire list, which makes it a good one to practice your painting and sealing technique on before committing to something more detailed.

![Handmade air dry clay star cluster charm, mini coffee cup charm, and organic wavy blob charm](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/star-coffee-blob-charms.webp)

## Sealing and Finishing, Properly

A charm that isn't sealed well won't survive daily wear on a bag. It'll pick up dirt, the paint can rub off at the edges, and any moisture exposure over time will soften the surface. A few options, depending on the finish you're after:

- **Two-part glossy resin** gives the most durable, professional-looking shine, but it takes more setup and a curing period.
- **UV resin** cures in minutes under a UV lamp and is a good middle ground for beginners.
- **Multiple thin coats of a dedicated sealant** are the lowest-effort option and still hold up reasonably well for charms that aren't getting heavy daily abuse.

Whichever you choose, seal both the front and back, and let each coat cure fully before attaching your jump ring and clasp hardware.

![Glossy resin being applied to a finished air dry clay charm for sealing](/images/easy-air-dry-clay-crafts-17-cute-clay-bag-charms/sealing-finishing.webp)

## Making Them Last

A few small habits will keep your charms looking good for a lot longer:

- Avoid leaving finished charms in direct sun for extended periods; some pigments can shift slightly over time.
- If a charm gets visibly dirty, wipe it gently with a barely damp cloth rather than soaking it.
- Store charms you're not using flat in a small box rather than tangled together in a drawer, so the paint and sealant don't rub against each other.
- If you're gifting a set, attach each charm to its own small clasp or lobster claw so it's ready to go straight onto a bag.

## A Small Sculptural Craft With a Lot of Personality

What makes this project worth the afternoon isn't really the individual charms, it's the fact that no two people's sets ever turn out the same. Two people using the exact same cutter for the same daisy charm will still end up with slightly different results, and that's the appeal. Start with two or three shapes from this list, get comfortable with the shaping-drying-painting-sealing rhythm, and build out a small collection from there. Before long you'll have a drawer full of tiny finished pieces and a bag that looks like nobody else's.
