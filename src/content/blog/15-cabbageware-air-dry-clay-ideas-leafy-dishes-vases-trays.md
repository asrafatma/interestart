Pinterest's 2026 predictions crowned an unlikely star: the cabbage. The report's tagline was "Live, laugh, leaf," and decor writers have tied the idea to cabbageware, the ruffled, leaf-shaped plates, bowls, vases, and trays that go back to 18th- and 19th-century European potters and had a revival in the 1960s. The look is playful but soft, usually set against neutral linen and wood, with earthy purples, greens, and whites.

The good news for makers is that you do not need a kiln or a wheel to try it. A fresh cabbage, a rolling pin, and a block of air dry clay get you most of the way. This list collects 15 cabbageware ideas that a beginner can finish at a table, plus the pressing, drying, and sealing steps that keep air dry clay from cracking.

## What You'll Need

**Essentials:**
- A block of air dry clay (white or terracotta, both work)
- A fresh cabbage, ideally a savoy, since its crinkled leaves carry deep veins
- A rolling pin and a small sharp knife or craft blade
- A few small bowls to use as drying forms, plus plastic wrap to line them
- A damp sponge, a cup of water, and a little cornstarch for dusting

**For finishing:**
- Fine sandpaper in two grits
- Acrylic paints in cream, sage green, and a dusty purple
- A soft brush for painting and a clear water-based varnish or a mix of PVA glue and water

![Overhead flat lay of a savoy cabbage, a block of white air dry clay, a rolling pin, small bowls lined with plastic wrap, a craft knife, sandpaper, and small pots of acrylic paint on a linen cloth](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/cabbageware-materials-flatlay.webp)

## Choose and Prep Your Cabbage

Savoy cabbage gives the deepest, most ruffled impression, which is why it is the classic choice for this look. Red cabbage has smoother, waxier leaves with bold veins, and it is a useful color reference when you reach the painting stage. Napa cabbage has long, pleated leaves that suit tray shapes.

Pick a firm head with large outer leaves and peel them off one at a time as you need them, keeping the rest in the fridge so they stay crisp. A limp leaf makes a soft, blurry impression, so use leaves the same day you peel them.

![Three cabbages side by side on a wooden board, a crinkled savoy, a smooth red cabbage, and a pleated napa, each with one leaf peeled back to show its veins](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/cabbage-varieties-leaf-textures.webp)

## Press a Real Cabbage Leaf Into the Clay

This is the technique behind almost every idea below. Roll your clay into an even slab, roughly 5 to 10 millimeters thick, since an even thickness keeps the piece strong. Peel off a large outer leaf, wipe it dry, and place it vein side down on the slab. Dust the leaf lightly with cornstarch if it tends to stick.

Roll over the leaf once or twice with moderate pressure, then peel it away slowly. The clay keeps a detailed impression of every vein and crinkle. Trim around the shape with your knife, following the leaf's natural outline or cutting your own.

![Close-up of a savoy cabbage leaf being peeled away from a slab of white clay, leaving a detailed impression of veins and crinkles](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/cabbage-leaf-impression-technique.webp)

## Shape It Over a Bowl and Ruffle the Edge

Line a small bowl with plastic wrap, then drape your pressed leaf shape inside it and gently ease it into the curve. Pinch and fold the outer edge in small waves to make the ruffled rim, and smooth any cracks with a damp fingertip or a dab of slip, which is clay softened with a little water.

Let it dry in the bowl for about a day, then lift it out and flip it so the underside dries evenly. Add water to slip gradually, since too much too fast weakens the clay.

![Pressed clay leaf shape draped inside a plastic-wrap-lined bowl with the edge being pinched into small ruffled waves](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/draping-and-ruffling-clay-leaf.webp)

## Dry Slowly to Avoid Cracks

Air dry clay typically takes 24 to 48 hours to dry fully. Keep pieces out of direct sun, away from heaters, and out of the oven, since fast, uneven drying is the main cause of cracks. Flip each piece at around the 24-hour mark. Once it is completely hard, sand the edges lightly with the coarser grit, then the finer one.

![Several leaf-pressed clay pieces drying on a wire cooling rack on a table, one upside down, away from direct sunlight](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/drying-rack-flip-clay-pieces.webp)

## Score and Slip: Joining Pieces Without Cracks

Any time two pieces of clay need to join, such as the leaf layers on the sculpture or the back plate on the wall pocket, score both surfaces with the tip of your knife in a light crosshatch, brush on a little slip, and press them together firmly. Smooth the seam with a damp finger. Pieces that are only pressed together can separate as they dry, and a little craft glue can reinforce a join once the clay is hard.

![Close-up of a craft knife scoring a crosshatch pattern on a clay surface beside a small dish of slip and a brush](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/scoring-and-slip-joining.webp)

## Trinkets and Desk Pieces

Small pieces are the best place to start because they dry quickly and forgive mistakes.

**1. A Cabbage Leaf Ring Dish**
Press a single small inner leaf, curl up the edges slightly, and let it dry in a small cup. It is the fastest project here and a good test of the technique.

**2. A Ruffled-Edge Trinket Tray**
Press a larger leaf, then pinch the rim into waves all the way around. Keep the center flat so keys or earrings sit securely.

![Two small cream-colored air dry clay trinket dishes with pressed leaf veins and ruffled edges holding a ring and a pair of earrings](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/ring-dish-and-trinket-tray.webp)

**3. A Pinch-Pot Cabbage Bud**
Shape a ball of clay into a small bowl by pinching, then add overlapping petal-like leaf layers around the outside, like a cabbage bud just beginning to open.

**4. A Layered Pen Holder**
Wrap leaf-pressed clay strips around a cardboard tube that you leave in place to hold its shape, overlapping each layer slightly for a tiered look.

![A small air dry clay cabbage-bud bowl made of overlapping leaf layers, next to a layered clay pen holder holding paintbrushes](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/cabbage-bud-bowl-and-pen-holder.webp)

## Wall and Display Pieces

These are made to be looked at, so you can be as ornate as you like.

**5. A Hanging Leaf Plate**
Press a large leaf, add a small hole near the top with a straw before drying, and hang it with a loop of twine.

**6. A Set of Three Nesting Display Dishes**
Press three leaves in graduated sizes and stack them for display, each one slightly smaller than the one beneath it.

![Three air dry clay leaf-pressed dishes in graduated sizes stacked on a wooden shelf beside a small plant](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/nesting-leaf-display-dishes.webp)

**7. A Leaf Wall Pocket**
Fold a pressed leaf shape into a cone and attach it flat-backed to a clay backplate with slip. It holds a few dried stems.

![A pressed clay leaf plate hanging on twine beside a small leaf-shaped wall pocket holding dried grasses on a plain wall](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/hanging-plate-and-wall-pocket.webp)

**8. A Cabbage Head Sculpture**
Roll a ball of clay about the size of a fist, then wrap pressed leaves around it one layer at a time, overlapping and curling the edges outward as you go. It makes a striking centerpiece.

![A hand-built air dry clay cabbage head sculpture made of layered pressed leaves with curled edges, on a wooden tray](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/cabbage-head-sculpture.webp)

## Table Decor That Isn't for Food

Air dry clay is not food safe, so keep these pieces for decoration only.

**9. A Leaf-Edged Tray for Candlesticks**
Press a wide oval leaf for a base and add a ruffled rim. Use it under an LED candle rather than a live flame.

**10. Cabbage Napkin Rings**
Shape short rings from pressed strips and press a tiny curled edge on each. A set of four looks lovely on a linen table.

**11. A Leaf Place-Card Holder**
Fold a small leaf shape over a flat base so it holds a card upright.

![Four small leaf-textured clay napkin rings on rolled linen napkins on a wooden table beside a leaf-shaped place card holder](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/napkin-rings-and-place-card-holder.webp)

**12. A Dried-Flower Vase**
Build a leaf-wrapped vessel around a small glass tube or jar that stays inside, then fill it with dried stems. Clay is not waterproof, so skip fresh flowers and water.

![A leaf-wrapped air dry clay vase holding dried pampas grass and wheat stems on a linen runner](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/dried-flower-vase-clay.webp)

## Paint and Finish

Color is where a plain cream leaf becomes cabbageware.

**13. A Soft White With Dry-Brushed Green Edges**
Paint the whole piece cream or soft white, let it dry, then lightly dry-brush sage green along the ruffled edges so the color catches on the raised veins.

**14. A Red-Cabbage Purple Colorway**
Start with a pale lilac base and dry-brush deeper dusty purple into the veins and outer rim. A damp sponge softens the transition.

![Two painted air dry clay cabbage leaf dishes: one soft white with sage green edges, one lilac with dusty purple veins, beside small paint pots](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/painted-cabbageware-colorways.webp)

**15. A Glossy Glaze-Look Finish**
Seal the finished piece with several thin coats of satin or gloss water-based varnish, drying fully between each one. It mimics the shine of a glaze without a kiln.

## Seal After It's Completely Dry

Only seal once the piece is fully dry. Options include a clear water-based varnish, an acrylic gloss medium, or a mix of equal parts PVA glue and water. Several thin coats work better than one thick one. Sealing helps protect against dust and light moisture, but it does not make air dry clay waterproof.

![Close-up of a soft brush applying a thin coat of clear varnish to a painted clay cabbage leaf dish](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/sealing-clay-leaf-dish.webp)

## If You Want to Serve Food

Air dry clay breaks down when it touches wet food, and sealants do not reliably fix that. For dishes that will hold food, use fired stoneware or porcelain finished with a glaze labeled food safe. A community pottery studio can fire pieces you hand-build, so you can use these same leaf-pressing techniques with real ceramic clay.

![A glazed stoneware leaf-shaped bowl with ruffled edges and an unglazed foot ring holding fresh fruit on a wooden table](/images/15-cabbageware-air-dry-clay-ideas-leafy-dishes-vases-trays/fired-glazed-stoneware-leaf-bowl.webp)

## Style Your Cabbageware

Cabbageware looks best when it has room to stand out. Group pieces in odd numbers, mix heights, and set them against plain linen, wood, or a neutral wall so the ruffles and veins do the talking. Add a few dried stems for softness and keep one or two pieces plain cream to give the eye a place to rest. Keep clay pieces away from sinks and steamy spots, since air dry clay is sensitive to moisture.

## Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

- **Rolling the slab too thin.** Thin edges crack and snap. Keep the thickness even.
- **Drying in the sun or near heat.** Fast drying warps and cracks pieces. Dry them slowly at room temperature.
- **Painting before it is fully dry.** Damp clay swells under paint and can crack the finish later.
- **Using air dry clay for food.** Keep these pieces decorative.

## Which Idea Should You Start With?

Start with the ring dish. It is small, quick, and teaches the pressing and drying steps in one sitting. After that, try the nesting display dishes, which build on the same technique at a larger scale. Save the cabbage head sculpture for last, since layering many leaves around a form is the most patient project on the list.

## Final Thoughts

Cabbageware works because it is a little silly and very beautiful at the same time, and you can make your own from a vegetable in the kitchen. Press one leaf, let it dry slowly, and paint it in two colors. The ruffled edge does most of the charm for you.
